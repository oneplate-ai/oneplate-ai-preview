Digestible AI · Plate Seven

Why Can an AI Answer
Be Wrong?

AI can write natural-sounding sentences, but a confident tone does not automatically make the facts correct.
This lesson explains hallucinations and four simple ways to check an answer before relying on it.

A hallucination is a plausible but false answer. OpenAI guidance

Hallucination is the term used when an AI presents information that is not true as if it were true.
It may invent or distort dates, people, book titles, quotations, or source links, and it may choose one confident answer when a question is unclear.

“Hallucination” does not mean that an AI is seeing something like a person does.
It is a shorthand for a natural-sounding answer whose content does not match the facts.

Why might AI answer even when it does not know?

A language model generates an answer by using patterns in the text that comes before it.
Producing a smooth sentence and checking whether its facts are true are not the same ability.

OpenAI’s research explanation says that language models can be rewarded for guessing rather than acknowledging uncertainty when they are trained and evaluated.
That can contribute to confident wrong answers when a model does not have a reliable answer.
Read OpenAI’s research explanation

A natural-sounding answer is not the same as a verified fact.

Check these answers especially carefully.

  • Today’s prices, stock, opening hours, or other frequently changing details.
  • Reservations, schedules, and supported regions that depend on current status.
  • Medical, legal, financial, or safety information where an error could cause harm.
  • Quotations, papers, books, laws, or statistics that require an original source.

An AI may show a source link, but that is not the end of the check.
You still need to see whether the link opens, whether the original text supports the claim, and whether it applies to your situation.

Use these four steps to check an answer.

First, choose the claims that need checking.
Instead of investigating every sentence equally, start with dates, numbers, names, quotations, and other details that would matter if wrong.

Second, find the original source.
Prefer the official agency, product documentation, or original paper that first published the information.

Third, compare the source with the answer.
Check whether the original has a narrower scope, an exception, a condition, or a different date that the AI left out.

Fourth, pause if you cannot verify it.
If you cannot find the original source or reliable sources disagree, do not use the answer immediately for an important decision.

Asking AI to check itself can help, but human review still matters.

You can ask an AI to say when it is unsure, attach a source to each claim, or mark statements that need verification.
Those requests can help you find places to review.

They do not remove the possibility of error.
You still need to compare the suggested sources with the original documents, and you should not hand an important decision over to the AI.

One-line takeaway.

A confident AI tone is not fact-checking, so compare important dates, numbers, and conditions with the original source yourself.
