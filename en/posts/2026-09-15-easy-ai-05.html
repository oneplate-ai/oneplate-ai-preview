Common Words You Meet
When Using AI

When you use services such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude, English AI terms often appear on the screen.
Here is a short guide to recognizing the words and connecting them to situations you can use right away.

You do not need to memorize these words.

Using AI well is less about knowing many technical words and more about clearly describing what you need and checking important answers.
It is enough to recognize the words below when they appear in a service description or settings screen.

AI and generative AI.

AI (Artificial Intelligence) is a broad term for technology that helps computers perform tasks that once needed human judgment.
A photo app finding people or objects, a map app recommending a route, and a phone turning speech into text can all involve AI.

Generative AI is AI that creates new outputs such as text, images, sound, or code from a request.
Pasting meeting notes into ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude and asking, “Summarize the key points in three lines,” or asking an image tool to make a picture are examples of using generative AI.
Those services are familiar examples that use generative AI; they are not the whole category.
Read Google’s official generative AI glossary

Models and services.

A model is the underlying technology an AI uses to produce an answer or result.
For example, you may see a menu for choosing a model in ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude; it is a choice of the underlying technology that will produce the answer within that service.

A service is the website or app interface and features that make a model usable in practice.
ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude are examples of services where people enter a conversation, upload a file, and receive a result.
Think of a model as a recipe and a service as a restaurant where you can order and receive food.
One service can use several models, and one model can be used in more than one service.

Prompts and context.

A prompt is the question, instruction, or material you give an AI.
The sentence “Summarize this article in three sentences” that you type into ChatGPT or Gemini is a prompt.
Adding “Use examples a child can understand” can bring the answer closer to what you want.

Context is the flow of earlier conversation and current input that an AI can refer to while making an answer.
For example, after asking Claude about a travel plan, you might say, “Change the second day to places we can visit even if it rains.”
The destination and schedule you discussed earlier are the context.
The same sentence in a new chat may get a different answer because the earlier conversation is not there.

Tokens and a context window.

A token is a small unit an AI uses to process text.
A token is not exactly the same as a character or word, but it is a term you often see next to “long document processing” in a ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude plan or model description.
For example, token counts may be mentioned when explaining whether you can paste a long report into one conversation.

A context window is the range of information an AI can consider together in one conversation.
If you have a very long conversation with Gemini or Claude and need to explain an early document again, the system may no longer be able to fully refer to that early part within its context window.
It is safer to paste important requirements, tables, and sources again when needed.

A hallucination is a plausible but false answer.

A hallucination happens when an AI creates information that is not true but sounds convincing.
For example, when asked for the author or source of a book that does not exist, ChatGPT might provide a title, name, and link that look real.
It can happen when a system guesses instead of saying that it does not know something.

For dates, numbers, quotations, and sources—and for important topics such as health, law, or finance—do not treat an AI answer as the final check.
Verify it in an original source or with an official organization.
It can also help to ask AI to say when it is unsure or to include sources.
Read OpenAI’s explanation of hallucinations

These are enough to remember at first.

  • AI (Artificial Intelligence): A broad term for technology that helps with tasks people do
  • Generative AI: AI that creates new text, images, code, and other outputs
  • Model: The underlying technology that produces an AI result
  • Service: A website or app that makes a model usable
  • Prompt: The question or instruction you enter
  • Context: The conversation flow an AI can refer to
  • Token: A small unit an AI uses to process text
  • Context window: The range of information an AI can consider together
  • Hallucination: A plausible answer that may not be true

In one sentence.

You do not need to memorize every AI term.
When you see these English words on screen, connect a model with underlying technology, a prompt with your request, context with the flow of conversation, and a hallucination with a convincing error that needs checking.
