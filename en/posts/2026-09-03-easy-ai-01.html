What Is AI? A Technology We Meet Every Day

For people meeting AI for the first time, let’s begin with the kinds of AI we already use in everyday life.

Let’s start with what AI means.

AI is short for artificial intelligence.
It refers to technologies designed to help computers carry out tasks that usually require human intelligence.

Those tasks can include understanding language, recognizing images, making judgments from information, and making predictions.
Google Cloud describes AI as a set of technologies that enables computers to learn, reason, and perform advanced tasks such as understanding language, analyzing data, and making helpful suggestions.
Read Google Cloud’s official introduction to AI

That does not mean AI has a human mind or feelings.
It is more useful to think of AI as a system that finds patterns in information and performs a given task, rather than as something that feels and thinks like a person.

AI is already around us.

When people hear “AI,” they may first think of ChatGPT or image-generation tools.
But AI had already been part of many everyday services long before those tools became familiar.

  • When a video service recommends something you might like
  • When a navigation app suggests a route that avoids traffic
  • When a phone finds faces or text in a photo
  • When an email service filters spam
  • When a keyboard predicts the next word you may type

What these examples share is that a computer uses information to make a judgment, classification, or prediction.

How does AI make a judgment?

Consider spam email.
An AI system does not “dislike” a message.
Instead, it can examine words, senders, and writing patterns that often appeared in messages previously labeled as spam, then calculate how likely a new message is to be spam.

That can be useful, but it does not guarantee the right answer every time.
If the information used for training is limited or a new situation appears, the result can be different or wrong.

Think of AI as a tool.

AI is less like magic that completely replaces people and more like a tool that can help people find, organize, and try things more quickly.

People still need to set the goal, check the result, and make important decisions.

In one sentence.

AI is technology that helps computers perform tasks that need human intelligence, and it is already part of everyday life through recommendations, search, and autocomplete.

In the next course, we will look at the difference between machine learning, a way for AI to learn from data, and generative AI, which creates outputs such as text, images, and code.
