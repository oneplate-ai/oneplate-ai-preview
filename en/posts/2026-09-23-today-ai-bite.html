Daily AI Bite

September 23, 2026

This issue looks at lower-cost AI models, model choice in developer tools, and broader ways for local governments to hear from residents.
As capabilities expand, cost, access controls, and human review still need attention.

1. OpenAI introduced GPT-6 Sol and Luna. OpenAI announcement

On September 22, OpenAI announced Sol and Luna as new models in the GPT-6 family.
They were presented as a faster and more affordable application of the technology behind GPT-6 Astra, and OpenAI said API prices are 50% lower than its promotional GPT-5.6 prices.

GPT-6 Sol is described as a balanced model for difficult work and coding, while Luna is positioned for smaller and faster tasks.
The performance and cost comparisons OpenAI published are the company’s own evaluations, not independent proof of superiority over other models.

  • Who it may help: Developers building AI features through an API and teams doing repeated coding or knowledge-work tasks.
  • Current status: OpenAI says the models are available in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu users, while Free and Go users can access Luna in the desktop app. The API names are gpt-6-sol and gpt-6-luna, with a gradual rollout.
  • Why it matters: Model selection is increasingly about usage and cost as well as raw capability.
  • What to watch: Actual access and limits may depend on the account, product, and rollout stage, so pricing and benchmark claims should be checked again before publication.

2. GitHub Copilot added GPT-6 Sol, Luna, and Claude Opus 5.5 as model choices. GitHub announcement

On September 22, GitHub announced that OpenAI’s GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna were available in Copilot.
GitHub describes Sol as a balanced model for interactive and agentic coding, and Luna as a cost-efficient option for smaller, faster tasks.

GitHub also announced on the same day that Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 was available in Copilot.
Users can choose a model in the model picker, but they still need to check usage-based billing and plan conditions for each option.

  • Who it may help: Developers using GitHub Copilot and organizations managing it for teams.
  • Current status: Sol is available to Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise plans; Luna to Pro, Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise; and Opus 5.5 to Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise. All are rolling out gradually, with availability varying by plan.
  • Why it matters: AI coding tools can be adjusted to task difficulty, speed, and cost instead of relying on one fixed model.
  • What to watch: More model choices do not remove the need for human review of code quality, security, and licensing, and usage-based charges may apply.

3. Google.org and Jigsaw are supporting an AI program for analyzing civic feedback. Google announcement

On September 22, Google.org announced support for a new Jigsaw partner program that helps local governments use Sensemaking AI.
The open-source tool suite is presented as a way to gather large-scale public feedback and surface common needs or themes for policy discussions.

Google said feedback from 8,000 residents in Bowling Green, Kentucky, informed a 25-year growth plan.
This does not mean AI makes policy decisions; it means AI can help people organize and compare a large amount of public input.

  • Who it may help: Local governments, public agencies, and teams analyzing community feedback.
  • Current status: The Jigsaw Partner Program invites local governments to express interest, and Google says they can run large-scale civic conversations at no operational cost.
  • Why it matters: AI can serve as an organizing layer when survey responses, comments, and other public input are too dispersed for people to review quickly.
  • What to watch: An AI summary cannot be assumed to represent every resident fairly, so people still need to check the source data, participation bias, and final policy decisions.
