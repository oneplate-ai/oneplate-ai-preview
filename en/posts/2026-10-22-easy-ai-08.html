The Context Window
Is AI’s Workspace.

AI can use earlier messages and documents, but the information it can consider at one time has limits.
This lesson explains context windows and how to handle a long conversation more reliably.

A context window is the workspace an AI can use at one time. Google documentation

Context window means the range of input and conversation that an AI can use when creating one response.
It is useful to think of it as short-term memory or a workspace that holds the material needed for the current answer.

The material is not limited to the latest question.
Depending on the service and model, it can include earlier messages, pasted documents, images, and other input.

Tokens are the units used to measure that space.

Tokens are small units that an AI uses to process text and other inputs.
A word is not always one token; a word can be split into several tokens depending on the language and expression.

That is why context windows are often described in tokens rather than characters.
As your question and reference material grow, they use more of the workspace, while the response also needs room to be generated.

Older parts of a long conversation may be removed. OpenAI documentation

When a conversation and its material become larger than the context window, the full history cannot keep being included.
A service may shorten, summarize, or leave out older messages before generating the next response.

That is why a detail from the beginning of a conversation can become less effective later.
If an AI seems to forget an earlier agreement, the reason may be that the information is no longer included in the current input, not that the system has human-like memory.

A large document does not mean every part will be used equally well.

A large context window is useful when you need to work with a long document or several files at once.
But as the amount of material grows, it can become harder to find the important part, separate conflicting details, and connect the question to the right passage.

So “the model can accept the material” is not the same as “the model will use every part accurately.”
Google’s long-context guidance recommends providing only what is needed and placing a clear question after the reference material when appropriate.

Keep the workspace organized for a long task.

First, choose only the material you need.
Removing unrelated messages and documents makes it easier to focus on the current question.

Second, repeat the important constraints.
Briefly restate the goal, audience, deadline, and prohibitions near the end of a long request when they must not be missed.

Third, leave a checkpoint summary.
Have a person confirm the decisions and remaining work, then use that summary as the starting point for a new conversation when needed.

Fourth, compare important results with the original.
Even a large context window can still lead to a misunderstanding or omission, so check important numbers, conditions, and quotations against the source.

One-line takeaway.

A context window is the workspace an AI can use for a response, so long tasks work better when you restate the needed material and key constraints.
