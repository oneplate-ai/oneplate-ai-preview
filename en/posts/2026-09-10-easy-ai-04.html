Why Can the Same Question
Get Different Answers?

Have you asked the same question in more than one AI service and received answers with different lengths or structures?
Instead of deciding which one is always best, start by comparing whether an answer fits your request.

The same question can lead to different answers.

AI services can use different models, connected tools, and ways of producing answers.
Even in the same service, an answer can change depending on the earlier conversation and on how specifically a question is written.

That means a difference alone does not prove that one answer is wrong.
It is more useful to first ask whether the answer actually completed the task you requested.

Try comparing one question yourself.

To start, choose a light question that does not depend on today’s date.
Open a new conversation in several services and enter exactly the same request, such as this one.

I am going camping overnight for the first time.
Give me five essential things to bring, and explain why each one is needed in one sentence.

A new conversation matters because an earlier exchange can affect the next answer.
When you compare answers, these three checks are enough.

  • Did it follow the request? Check that it gives exactly five items and one-sentence reasons.
  • Is it easy for a beginner to understand? See whether it avoids unexplained jargon and helps you prepare right away.
  • Does it signal what needs checking? For changing information such as prices, weather, or rules, look for sources or a way to verify the claim.

Add one more line when the answer is not useful.

“Tell me what to bring” is a fine start.
But adding the situation and the shape of the answer can bring the result closer to what you need.
Google’s official prompt guidance introduces clear, specific instructions, constraints, and response formats as useful prompting strategies.
Read the official prompt guidance

Try changing the question just a little, like this.

I am going camping overnight for the first time on an autumn weekend.
Give me only five essential items in a numbered list.
Write one sentence explaining each item, and leave out optional gear.

This version includes the situation, the number of items, the response format, and what to leave out.
Instead of asking AI to guess what you have in mind, you are showing it the shape of the result you want.

What to remember while comparing.

A longer answer is not automatically more accurate, and a shorter answer is not automatically less helpful.
An answer that follows your request and is ready to use is a good starting point.

For important information such as health, law, finance, or today’s prices and weather, do not stop at comparing AI answers.
Check the dates, numbers, and names in an answer against official organizations or original sources.

In one sentence.

AI answers can differ even when the question is the same.
Rather than ranking services, compare whether an answer follows your request, is easy to understand, and signals information that needs checking.

This post was prepared after checking official prompt guidance from OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic.
The guidance commonly covers clear requests, desired output formats, and defining success criteria first.
